# A Return reverses the funding, and any deficit it leaves becomes Recovery Receivable

The Simulator knows NACHA return codes and their Return Windows (`railbench.common.returns`), checked on 2026-11-27 against nacha.org, whose pages confirm the two-day timeframe for ordinary returns and the 60-day extended timeframe for unauthorized consumer debits (R10 and R11, with a Written Statement of Unauthorized Debit; "Differentiating Unauthorized Return Reasons", effective 1 April 2021), while the exact deadline wording comes from Nacha rules excerpts seen in search results (the full Operating Rules and Nacha's 2026 proposed-rules PDF are behind a member login, so re-check before relying on it): R01, R02, R03, R04 and R29 must reach the ODFI by the opening of business on the second Banking Day following the Settlement Date, and unauthorized debits to a consumer account, R05, R07, R10 and R11, by the opening of business on the Banking Day following the 60th calendar day after it. SPEC §4.3 says "R10 within 60 calendar days"; the source is slightly later, the opening of the next Banking Day after day 60, and we follow it. "Opening of business" is midnight New York time, the same moment ACH funds become Available (ADR 0010), so the R01–R03 window closes exactly when they do and a Return at that moment still beats the availability job. A Return is a `return` Fault with a code and `at` (how long after settlement), or one injected now through the Simulator's `POST /payments/{id}/returns`; one outside its window is refused, by the Scenario file before the run, by the Simulator when it creates the planned Payment (the first moment it knows when it settles) and by the injection endpoint. Only a settled ACH pull returns: RTP has no Returns, and a returned Payout (an ACH push) isn't simulated, since the risk this project is about is the funding of a buyer's balance; the Ledger records a Return of a push as an exception. A Return is in the Fault Manifest, as `late_return` once the 2-Banking-Day window has passed and `ach_return` inside it, and delivery Faults act on its `payment.returned` event like any other (a duplicate can now hold its copies until a later event has gone, which is how a settlement is redelivered after its Return). In the Ledger `returned` follows `settled` or `available` and is final; a Return for a Payment the Ledger hasn't recorded as settled is impossible, an exception record with no Ledger write (SPEC §4.2's own example), even when only the settlement is late, because guessing a settlement time from a Return would book a Settlement no Provider Event reported. Applying a Return is one database transaction (ADR 0010), with the Provider Event as Actor and at its Effective Time: a posted Reversal of the funding's pending Transaction (ADR 0009: a Return of a settled Transaction is a posted Reversal, never before its Settlement), a posted Reversal of the settlement's sweep to Bank Cash, so the money visibly leaves Bank Cash and the Clearing Account still nets to zero, and the release of the availability Hold if it's still in place, so returned money is never made Available. We reverse the sweep rather than post a new Bank Cash Transaction because the sweep only happened because of the settlement that the Return undoes. If the Customer Balance is then short, its Posted Balance less unsettled outflows below zero because the money was spent (a lot bought at an Auction Close, or paid out), a posted Transaction moves the deficit to that Customer's own Recovery Receivable (`recovery-receivable:<Customer Balance>`, one asset Account per Customer per currency, opened on first use), bringing the Customer Balance back to zero, and a return exception (`payments.return_exceptions`, append-only, at most one per Payment) records the exposure in Minor Units with the Customer, Payment, return code, Inbox event, recovery Transaction and whether it was a Late Return; it's on `GET /payments/{id}` and `GET /return-exceptions`. Recovery Receivable per Customer, rather than one shared Account with attribution, keeps "what does this Customer owe us" a plain balance for Reconciliation (#9) and collections. We measure the deficit on Posted less unsettled outflows, not on Available: another pull's availability Hold is money the Customer has, and a payout in flight is money already leaving. A deficit is moved whenever a Return leaves one, also before the funds were Available (a buyer can spend unavailable funds at an Auction Close), so a Return never leaves a Customer Balance negative; only a Late Return can mean Mintwharf already paid the money out. The deficit is measured on everything the Ledger knows when it applies the Return, which for a delayed Return can include spending after its Effective Time. Recovery Receivable counts against the Customer as long as it's owed: the funds behind a Standard Payout count as Available only when its buyers' (and seller's) Available Balance less what they owe in Recovery Receivable isn't negative, so a Standard Payout behind a returned buyer goes on waiting even though their Customer Balance is back to zero, and a seller's Instant Payout whose buyer was returned is back in their outstanding exposure, against their limit, until that buyer funds what they owe. Collecting Recovery Receivable (netting it against a later deposit, or writing it off) and raising return exceptions in the Exception Queue are left to Reconciliation and its Proposals (#9, #11).
