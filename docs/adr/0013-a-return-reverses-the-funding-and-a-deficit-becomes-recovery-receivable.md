# A Return reverses the funding, and any deficit it leaves becomes Recovery Receivable

Return Windows follow nacha.org, checked 2026-11-27: R01, R02, R03, R04 and R29 by the opening of the second Banking Day after the Settlement Date; unauthorized consumer debits R05, R07, R10 and R11 by the opening of the Banking Day after the 60th calendar day (the exact wording is in the member-only Operating Rules, so re-check it). SPEC §4.3's "R10 within 60 calendar days" is slightly short of that, and we follow the source. "Opening" is midnight New York, the moment ACH funds become Available (ADR 0010), and a Late Return is one after that moment, decided by time in both the Simulator's manifest and the Ledger, never by whether the availability job has run. The Simulator returns only settled ACH pulls (RTP has none; a Payout's Return isn't simulated, and the Ledger's state machine refuses one), as planned by a `return` Fault or injected through `POST /payments/{id}/returns`, and refuses a Return outside its window in the Scenario file, at creation and at injection. In the Ledger `returned` follows `settled` or `available` and is final; a Return before the Ledger recorded a settlement is an exception with no Ledger write (SPEC §4.2), rather than a guessed Settlement. Applying a Return, in one database transaction with the Provider Event as Actor at its Effective Time T, posts a Reversal of the funding and of its sweep to Bank Cash (so Clearing nets to zero and Bank Cash shows the money leaving; ADR 0009) and releases the availability Hold if it's still in place. Then it walks the Customer Balance from T through every later Effective Time at which its Posted Balance or unsettled outflows change, as known now, and wherever Posted less unsettled outflows is below zero it posts a Transaction moving the shortfall to that Customer's Recovery Receivable (`recovery-receivable:<Customer Balance>`, an asset Account per Customer per currency) at that moment, each with a return exception carrying the exposure in Minor Units. The invariant that holds is exact: once a Return with Effective Time T is applied, at every Effective Time from T on, as known at that moment, the Customer Balance's Posted Balance less unsettled outflows is not negative, and Recovery Receivable holds exactly the moves that made it so. Moves only top up, so a later inflow never collects the receivable, and Entries recorded after the Return is applied are outside the guarantee: a later overdrawing purchase is an ordinary unfunded purchase (ADR 0012), not moved. Holds don't count (another pull's availability Hold is money the Customer has), and neither does the time before T. Returns on one Customer Balance take a lock per Customer Balance, so two at once each see the other's Reversal. What a Customer owes in Recovery Receivable counts against their Available Balance for Payouts: a Standard Payout behind a returned buyer goes on waiting, and an Instant Payout whose buyer was returned is back on its seller's limit, until the buyer funds what they owe. We chose a per-Customer Recovery Receivable over one shared Account so "what does this Customer owe" stays a plain balance. Collecting it, and raising return exceptions in the Exception Queue, are left to Reconciliation (#9, #11).
