# A retry must match exactly, and a Reversal is final

An Idempotency Key promises "this exact request, once". The payload fingerprint covers everything the caller sends except the key itself: the Entries, the Actor, the Effective Time (compared as an instant, so offsets don't matter) and the description. A retry that differs in any of them, even only in its description, is a 409 Conflict, not a replay: a caller that reuses a key for a request it changed has a bug, and silently returning the old Transaction would hide it. A Transaction can be reversed once (a unique `reverses_id`), and a Reversal can't itself be reversed. To undo a Reversal, post a new Transaction with its own Actor. Reversal chains would make "is this Transaction in effect?" depend on how many links follow it, and a fresh Transaction says plainly who restored the money and why. The `railbench.migrating` setting that lets migrations past the append-only trigger only guards against accidents, not against a determined owner, who can disable a trigger anyway. The real boundary is access control: the app role has no `UPDATE` or `DELETE` grant (ADR 0006).
