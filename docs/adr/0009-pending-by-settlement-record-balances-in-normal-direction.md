# Pending money settles by an appended Settlement, and balances read in the Account's normal direction

A Transaction is posted or pending, fixed when it's posted, and a pending one becomes Posted when a **Settlement** row naming it is appended, with its own Effective Time, Recorded Time, Actor and Idempotency Key; no Entry is changed or re-posted, so ACH can go in pending at origination and settle later (ADR 0004) and "was it settled on Tuesday, as known on Tuesday?" is the same bitemporal question as for Entries. A Reversal takes its original's status and settles with it: reversing an unsettled pending Transaction (a decline) cancels it while still pending, and reversing a settled one (a Return) is posted. We rejected booking settlement as a second, offsetting Transaction, because it doubles the Entries and needs a matching rule to say which pending Entries it cleared. Balances are reported in each Account's normal direction (raw debit-positive sum for assets and expenses, negated for liabilities and revenue), so a Customer Balance holding $30 reads +3,000 and Available = Posted − Holds − unsettled outflows reads the way the glossary says; Entries themselves stay signed debit-positive. Unsettled outflows are the pending Transactions, each netted with its Reversal, that take money out of the Account. Pending inflows don't add to Available until they settle. A **Hold** is an appended row that ends at most once (a unique `hold_id`) by a release or by a capture, and a capture posts the Transaction that moves the money in the same commit, at that Transaction's Effective Time. Every bound is inclusive: a row counts when its Effective Time ≤ T and its Recorded Time ≤ T′, and T′ can't be after now. There's no balance cache: balances are derived in SQL on every read, using an index on Entries by (Account, Effective Time, Recorded Time). A cache would need its own rebuild and a test checking it against this derivation, and we'll only add one if the derivation turns out too slow.
