# Settlement Reports are evidence, and only a Provider Adjustment on one is booked

Each Provider publishes one Settlement Report per Banking Day as CSV, header row always (so a missing report is never mistaken for a quiet day), at 20:00 New York, after every Rail's Cutoff. The contract both sides share lives in `railbench.common.settlement_reports`, like the NACHA rules in `railbench.common.returns`: the columns (report date, provider, line ID, type `settlement` or `adjustment`, the Provider's Payment ID, the Idempotency Key the Ledger originated it with as the originator's reference, Rail, direction, currency, signed integer amount in Minor Units, + into and - out of Mintwharf's account at the Provider, `settled_at` as a UTC instant, and for an adjustment the original line ID and an `AdjustmentReason`), and the Cutoff per Provider × Rail (fictional: ACH 16:00, RTP 18:00 New York). A settlement counts toward the Banking Day it happened on if that is a Banking Day and it happened before its Rail's Cutoff, otherwise toward the next, so RTP settled after 18:00, on a weekend or on a Fed holiday lands on the next Banking Day's report; because the rule is published, Reconciliation (#9) can expect the same day and an ordinary after-Cutoff settlement is no Break. Lines are ordered by `settled_at`, then line ID, and line IDs are hashes of the Idempotency Key, so a Scenario and seed give byte-identical reports. One report covers every Payment its Provider settled that day and is frozen once published, so a second Scenario run in the same database would find its days' reports already published, and ingested, without its settlements, and Reconciliation would see false missing lines: determinism therefore holds per fresh database, and the runner refuses one that already holds Ledger or Simulator rows (amending ADR 0011, which promised it whatever else the database held). We rejected a per-run Provider, which would fake the one-report-per-Provider shape the SPEC asks for. A Return isn't on a report: it reaches the Ledger as a Provider Event, and the settlement line stays on its day. The Simulator builds each report from what it settled (`railbench.simulator.reports`, pure), freezes it as published, and injects five report Faults there, each in the Fault Manifest when the report it shows on is published, with the line ID as its event and its kind as its cause code: `missing_report_line` (the line is left off), `wrong_report_amount` (off by `by`), `settlement_after_cutoff` (the Provider books a settlement its Provider Event said happened earlier at that day's Cutoff, so the line, stamped with the Cutoff, lands on the next Banking Day's report: a timing disagreement, even on the eve of a Fed holiday, while an ordinary late settlement agrees), `provider_adjustment`, and `orphan_adjustment` (a fee correction against a Payment ID the Provider never made for Mintwharf, with no Idempotency Key: an adjustment nothing in the Ledger expects). We rejected making the after-Cutoff Fault move the settlement itself, because the published rule would then put it on the same day the Ledger expects and nothing would disagree. The Ledger gets reports only through the Provider interface (ADR 0005): the `settlement-report-ingest@1.0.0` job, on the Ledger's Clock after ACH availability and Payout release, fetches each due day's report from the first Banking Day it made a Payment on until one is ingested, verifies it like a webhook (the same HMAC header over the body, ADR 0010; a bad one is logged and stored nowhere), and receives it in one database transaction under a lock on (Provider, day): stored raw with its headers, unique by (Provider, day, SHA-256), so the same bytes again do nothing; a body the adapter can't read is kept `malformed`, and a different body for a day already ingested (a re-issue) is kept as a `conflict` with nothing re-ingested, since choosing between two versions is a Reconciliation decision, not the ingest's. The job never asks again for a day it has ingested, so today a re-issue only arrives through `SettlementReports.replay` (`_receive`); how a Provider announces one is for #9. An ingested report's lines are stored as stated, append-only like the Inbox, and change no balance: a missing, wrong or late line is evidence that Reconciliation turns into a Break, never something the Ledger corrects on its own. A Provider Adjustment, which the Provider reports only on the report (there is no Provider Event for it), is decided against the original Payment, found by the Provider's Payment ID, and each decision is appended to `provider_adjustments`, never updated: `unmatched`, for good, when there's no such Payment (an orphan), the line contradicts it (Idempotency Key, Rail, direction, currency, or a sign or reason the money can't move that way for it), the adjustments against it would exceed what settled, or it ended declined or canceled without settling, for Reconciliation to flag (SPEC §4.5); `pending` when it agrees but the Ledger hasn't recorded it settled yet (its `settled` Provider Event delayed or out of order), decided again under the Payment's lock at the start of every later run of the job until it's booked or unmatched, and left pending, visibly, while the Payment neither settles nor ends; otherwise `booked`, at most once per line (partial unique indexes allow one pending and one final decision per line). The two reasons move money in opposite directions; we read SPEC §4.5's "claws back part of a settled amount" as the Provider taking back part of what it moved, from whoever got it. A fee correction: the Provider takes the amount out of Mintwharf's account (line -), and Mintwharf bears it: Provider Fees (a new expense Account, `provider-fees:<currency>`, extending SPEC §3.1) debit, Clearing Account credit, then Clearing Account debit, Bank Cash credit; the Customer is untouched. A Payout recall (pushes only): the Provider pulls part of a Payout back from its recipient into Mintwharf's account (line +), so the seller wasn't paid that part and Mintwharf owes it to them again: Clearing Account debit, Customer Balance credit, then Bank Cash debit, Clearing Account credit, left Available with no automatic re-send, like a declined ACH push (ADR 0012); the seller ends where they'd be had that part never gone out, and Mintwharf is out nothing. Both are posted Transactions at the line's `settled_at`, keyed by the Payment and line ID, through the Payment's own Clearing Account, which nets to zero at once. Neither takes money from a Customer Balance, so neither can leave one short and Recovery Receivable (ADR 0013) never comes into it; a partial recall of a pull would, and we left it out, since ACH Returns are all or nothing. The Actor is the ingest job, and the `provider_adjustments` row names the report line, Payment and Transaction: we rejected a new Actor kind for a report line, which would change the Entries' Actor check, and rejected storing reports in the Inbox, which holds Provider Events. Replaying the Ledger (ADR 0011) runs the job's retry at each moment it decided a pending adjustment and receives every stored report body at the moment it was received, so the lines and decisions come out the same. An adjustment taking effect in a closed day waits for Daily Close (SPEC §3.4).
