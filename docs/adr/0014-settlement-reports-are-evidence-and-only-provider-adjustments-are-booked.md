# Settlement Reports are evidence, and only a Provider Adjustment on one is booked

Each Provider publishes one Settlement Report per Banking Day as CSV, header row always (so a missing report is never mistaken for a quiet day), at 20:00 New York, after every Rail's Cutoff. The contract both sides share lives in `railbench.common.settlement_reports`, like the NACHA rules in `railbench.common.returns`: the columns (report date, provider, line ID, type `settlement` or `adjustment`, the Provider's Payment ID, the Idempotency Key the Ledger originated it with as the originator's reference, Rail, direction, currency, signed integer amount in Minor Units, + into and - out of Mintwharf's account at the Provider, `settled_at` as a UTC instant, and for an adjustment the original line ID and a reason), and the Cutoff per Provider × Rail (fictional: ACH 16:00, RTP 18:00 New York). A settlement counts toward the Banking Day it happened on if that is a Banking Day and it happened before its Rail's Cutoff, otherwise toward the next, so RTP settled after 18:00, on a weekend or on a Fed holiday lands on the next Banking Day's report; because the rule is published, Reconciliation (#9) can expect the same day and an ordinary after-Cutoff settlement is no Break. Lines are ordered by `settled_at`, then line ID, and line IDs are hashes of the Idempotency Key, so a Scenario and seed give byte-identical reports; since one report covers everything the Provider settled that day, when a run ticks to publish one is the one thing that now depends on what else the database holds (ADR 0011), and the runner ticks at each report with anything on it, catching up the empty ones in between. A Return isn't on a report: it reaches the Ledger as a Provider Event, and the settlement line stays on its day. The Simulator builds each report from what it settled (`railbench.simulator.reports`, pure), freezes it as published, and injects four report Faults there, each in the Fault Manifest when the report it shows on is published, with the line ID as its event: `missing_report_line` (the line is left off), `wrong_report_amount` (off by `by`), `settlement_after_cutoff` (the Provider books a settlement its Provider Event said happened earlier at that day's Cutoff, so the line, stamped with the Cutoff, lands on the next Banking Day's report: a timing disagreement, even on the eve of a Fed holiday, while an ordinary late settlement agrees) and `provider_adjustment`. We rejected making the after-Cutoff Fault move the settlement itself, because the published rule would then put it on the same day the Ledger expects and nothing would disagree. The Ledger gets reports only through the Provider interface (ADR 0005): the `settlement-report-ingest@1.0.0` job, on the Ledger's Clock after ACH availability and Payout release, fetches each due day's report from the first Banking Day it made a Payment on until one is ingested, verifies it like a webhook (the same HMAC header over the body, ADR 0010; a bad one is logged and stored nowhere), and receives it in one database transaction under a lock on (Provider, day): stored raw with its headers, unique by (Provider, day, SHA-256), so the same bytes again do nothing; a body the adapter can't read is kept `malformed`, and a different body for a day already ingested (a re-issue) is kept as a `conflict` with nothing re-ingested, since choosing between two versions is a Reconciliation decision, not the ingest's. An ingested report's lines are stored as stated, append-only like the Inbox, and change no balance: a missing, wrong or late line is evidence that Reconciliation turns into a Break, never something the Ledger corrects on its own. A Provider Adjustment, which the Provider reports only on the report (there is no Provider Event for it), is booked at ingest against the original Payment, found by the Provider's Payment ID, when the line agrees with it (Idempotency Key, Rail, direction, currency), the Ledger recorded it settled, the reason fits and the adjustments booked against it stay within what settled; otherwise it's stored `unmatched` with why and books nothing, for Reconciliation to flag (SPEC §4.5). There are two reasons. A fee correction takes money out of Mintwharf's account and Mintwharf bears it: Provider Fees (a new expense Account, `provider-fees:<currency>`, extending SPEC §3.1) to the Clearing Account, then the Clearing Account to Bank Cash. A Payout recall brings part of a push back in, so the seller wasn't paid that part and Mintwharf owes it again: the Clearing Account to the Customer Balance, then Bank Cash to the Clearing Account, left Available with no automatic re-send, like a declined ACH push (ADR 0012). Both are posted Transactions at the line's `settled_at`, keyed by the Payment and line ID, through the Payment's own Clearing Account, which nets to zero at once. Neither takes money from a Customer Balance, so neither can leave one short and Recovery Receivable (ADR 0013) never comes into it; a partial recall of a pull would, and we left it out, since ACH Returns are all or nothing. The Actor is the ingest job, and the `provider_adjustments` row names the report line, Payment and Transaction: we rejected a new Actor kind for a report line, which would change the Entries' Actor check, and rejected storing reports in the Inbox, which holds Provider Events. Replaying the Ledger (ADR 0011) receives every stored report body at the moment it was received, after that moment's jobs, so the lines and adjustments come out the same. An adjustment taking effect in a closed day waits for Daily Close (SPEC §3.4).
