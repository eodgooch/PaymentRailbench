# Auction Closes book gross, lot by lot, and a Payout's money is reserved until it goes out

An Auction Close is booked in one database transaction by the `auction-close` system job, every read and write on that transaction's connection: each lot is one posted Transaction at the close, in which the buyer's Customer Balance pays the hammer price, the seller's is credited the price less Mintwharf's fee (`SELLER_FEE_BPS`, a fictional setting that extends SPEC §13, rounded down to the cent in the seller's favour) and Fee Revenue takes the fee. It's recorded once per auction ID in an append-only `marketplace` schema (a retry with other lots is a conflict), and every seller gets one Payout of what they're owed from it. We book gross, not net: a Customer who both bought and sold owes their purchases and is owed their sales, lot by lot, and must fund their purchases like any buyer, so each lot's money has exactly one source and one Auction Close reconciles for that Customer as plain lists; netting would make their sale proceeds the hidden funding of their purchases, so a Late Return on a third buyer (#7) would chain through them. Every Payout's amount is reserved at the close by a Hold on the seller's Customer Balance, Instant or Standard, and the Hold ends only when the money's fate is booked: the Payout's Payment names it, and the Transaction that books the money going out (an RTP push's settlement, an ACH push's pending Transaction at submission) captures it, so from the close until the money has left, the proceeds are never Available, to the seller or as Available money paying for what the seller bought. A declined RTP books nothing and its Hold stays for the Standard Payout it becomes; an ACH push declined after submission is a pending Reversal of the Transaction that captured the Hold, and one that ends before the Ledger heard it submitted has its Hold released by the release job, so either way the money stays Available in the Customer Balance, with no automatic retry. We considered placing the reservation only for Standard Payouts and releasing it on release, but then a buyer-and-seller's own outgoing proceeds counted as Available in three windows (an Instant Payout decided but not yet booked, a declined RTP before its fall-back, a released ACH push before its submission, which can last as long as the Provider takes), which let another seller's Payout go out early. The funds behind a Standard Payout count as Available when neither the buyers of its seller's lots in that auction nor the seller has a negative Available Balance, reservations included: a buyer who funded by ACH is clear when the Ledger releases their funding's availability Hold (ADR 0010), and the seller when their own purchases are paid for, so a buyer-and-seller who never funds what they bought isn't paid out at all rather than ending in debt. The test counts reservations because leaving them out would net the seller's proceeds against their purchases. We rejected linking each lot to named funding Payments, which needs the caller to say which pull paid for what and still has to handle a buyer who paid from an existing balance; the cost of the balance test is that a buyer's unfunded purchases elsewhere hold these Payouts up too, which errs on the safe side. A Payout waiting on a buyer who never funds waits silently, so its view names the Customer Balances it's waiting for; raising it as an exception in the Exception Queue is left to Reconciliation (#9). The `payout-release` job runs on the Ledger's Clock right after the ACH availability job and, one database transaction per step, appended at most once per Payout (`reserved`, `instant`, `standard`, `released`, `returned`), releases each due Standard Payout at its own Clock time and sends it as an ACH credit push, which mirrors the pull through the ACH Clearing Account: pending at submission (an unsettled outflow), then its Settlement and a posted Transaction from Bank Cash to the Clearing Account, which nets it to zero. We send Standard Payouts over ACH rather than RTP because that's what a Standard Payout is, and it puts the Night's Payouts on more than one Rail. An Instant Payout, which the seller asks for on the Auction Close, goes out over RTP straight after the close commits if the seller's outstanding exposure (their Instant Payouts not declined whose buyers' funds aren't Available yet, measured before this auction's lots are booked) plus this one is at most `INSTANT_PAYOUT_LIMIT_CENTS` and the seller's own Available Balance isn't negative once the auction is booked (Mintwharf doesn't send money at its own risk to a Customer who owes it); otherwise the whole amount falls back to a Standard Payout, since a split would give one sale two Payouts to reconcile. The exposure check and the decision run under an advisory lock per seller asking for an Instant Payout, taken in sorted order after the auction's own lock and held to the commit, so two auctions closing at once can't both spend one seller's limit. The Simulator's RTP is credit push only, settles or (when planned) is declined the moment it's created on any day at any hour, since RTP runs 24/7/365 and has no Cutoff or Banking Days, and emits that single Provider Event; the Ledger books a settled one as two posted Transactions (Customer Balance to the RTP Clearing Account, capturing the reservation, and Bank Cash to the Clearing Account, which nets it to zero at once), and its state machine is `created → settled | declined`, final: nothing follows either, there are no Returns, and any other RTP event is an exception. Duplicate and delayed delivery Faults work on RTP events as on ACH; an out-of-order Fault can't, since an RTP Payment emits one event. A declined Instant Payout becomes a Standard Payout rather than leaving the seller unpaid. Payments are originated after the commit that decides them, under Idempotency Keys derived from the auction and the seller, and the job sends any it finds unsent, so a Provider outage delays a Payout but never duplicates it; `POST /payments` makes only pulls. Replaying the Inbox reruns each Auction Close from its recorded lots, fee and limit, originates every Payment from its row (a Payout's naming its Hold as found in the new database), and runs the Payout job at each moment it recorded a step.

Amended by ADR 0015: the Exception Queue holds Breaks only, so a Payout waiting on unfunded buyers surfaces through the MCP server's tools (#11) for the triage agent (#16), not in the Queue.
