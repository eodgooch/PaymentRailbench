# Auction Closes book gross, lot by lot, and a Standard Payout waits until its buyers' money is Available

An Auction Close is booked in one database transaction by the `auction-close` system job: each lot is one posted Transaction at the close, in which the buyer's Customer Balance pays the hammer price, the seller's is credited the price less Mintwharf's fee (`SELLER_FEE_BPS`, a fictional setting that extends SPEC §13, rounded down to the cent in the seller's favour) and Fee Revenue takes the fee. It's recorded once per auction ID in an append-only `marketplace` schema (a retry with other lots is a conflict), and every seller gets one Payout of what they're owed from it. We book gross, not net: a Customer who both bought and sold owes their purchases and is owed their sales, lot by lot, and must fund their purchases like any buyer, so each lot's money has exactly one source and one Auction Close reconciles for that Customer as plain lists (what they bought against what they pulled in, what they sold less fees against what was paid out); netting would make their sale proceeds the hidden funding of their purchases, so a Late Return on a third buyer (#7) would chain through them. The funds behind a Payout are the buyers of its seller's lots in that auction, and they count as Available when none of those buyers has a negative Available Balance, which for a buyer who funded by ACH is when the Ledger releases the funding's availability Hold (ADR 0010); a Standard Payout puts a Hold on the seller's Customer Balance at the close, so the proceeds are Posted but not Available, and a buyer-and-seller's held proceeds can't make their own Available Balance look funded. We rejected linking each lot to named funding Payments, which needs the caller to say which pull paid for what and still has to handle a buyer who paid from an existing balance; the cost of the balance test is that a buyer's unfunded purchases elsewhere hold up these Payouts too, which errs on the safe side. The `payout-release` job runs on the Ledger's Clock right after the ACH availability job and, one database transaction per step, releases each due Standard Payout's Hold at its own Clock time and sends the Payout as an ACH credit push, which mirrors the pull: a pending Transaction from the Customer Balance to the ACH Clearing Account at submission (an unsettled outflow, so the money is no longer Available), then its Settlement and a posted Transaction from Bank Cash to the Clearing Account, which nets it to zero; a declined ACH push is a pending Reversal, leaving the money Available in the Customer Balance, with no automatic retry. We send Standard Payouts over ACH rather than RTP because that's what a Standard Payout is, and it puts the Night's Payouts on more than one Rail. An Instant Payout, which the seller asks for on the Auction Close, goes out over RTP straight after the close commits, if the seller's outstanding exposure (their Instant Payouts not declined whose funds aren't Available yet, measured before this auction's lots are booked) plus this one is at most `INSTANT_PAYOUT_LIMIT_CENTS`; otherwise the whole amount falls back to a Standard Payout, since a split would give one sale two Payouts to reconcile. The Simulator's RTP is credit push only, settles or (when planned) is declined the moment it's created on any day at any hour, since RTP runs 24/7/365 and has no Cutoff or Banking Days, and emits that single Provider Event; the Ledger books a settled one as two posted Transactions (Customer Balance to the RTP Clearing Account, Bank Cash to the Clearing Account, which nets to zero at once), and its state machine is `created → settled | declined`, final: nothing follows either, there are no Returns, and any other RTP event is an exception. Duplicate and delayed delivery Faults work on RTP events as on ACH; an out-of-order Fault can't, since an RTP Payment emits one event. A declined Instant Payout moved no money, so the job turns it into a Standard Payout (a Hold, then the usual release) rather than leaving the seller unpaid. Payments are originated after the commit that decides them, under Idempotency Keys derived from the auction and the seller, and the job sends any it finds unsent, so a Provider outage delays a Payout but never duplicates it; `POST /payments` makes only pulls. Replaying the Inbox reruns each Auction Close from its recorded lots, fee and limit, and the Payout job at each moment it recorded a step.
